## Length and Chapter Planning

A possible starting budget is 20 substantive chapters of approximately 7,500 words each, totaling 150,000 words before introductory and concluding material. The final chapter structure must follow the needs of the subject rather than force every course into identical headings.

For conservative quality control, target at least 150,000 words of substantive book content, excluding references and bibliographic entries. Count the text extracted from the final PDF as well as the manuscript, and resolve discrepancies before release. Do not use repeated passages, filler, or irrelevant material to reach the minimum.

Each book should be usable independently. Necessary explanations may recur across books, but each course must contribute its own distinct depth, examples, and applications.

## Per-Book Teaching Structure

Adapt the following elements to each subject:

1. Course orientation, learning objectives, and a conceptual map.
2. Foundations, key terminology, historical development, and major theoretical perspectives.
3. Step-by-step explanation of core concepts in accessible English.
4. Critical examination of research evidence, competing interpretations, and limitations.
5. Real-life applications and extended, clearly labeled fictional case examples.
6. Worked clinical, ethical, research, or organizational reasoning as appropriate to the course.
7. Original diagrams, decision pathways, comparison tables, and illustrative scenarios where useful.
8. Developmental, cultural, linguistic, family, school, and service-system considerations.
9. Reflection questions, applied exercises, and doctoral-level discussion tasks.
10. Chapter summaries, integrative applications, glossary, and verified references.

A diagram must teach something specific. Examples include developmental trajectories, family-system maps, assessment pathways, functional-behavior sequences, multilevel data structures, and coordinated-care workflows. Every figure needs a number, caption, source or original-authorship note, and an explanation in the surrounding text. Visuals must remain legible in the PDF and must not be replaced by vague instructions to imagine them.

## Production Stages for Each Book

1. **Subject mapping:** Expand the supplied coverage into a detailed chapter outline and evidence-search plan.
2. **Evidence preparation:** Identify and check reliable scholarly and professional sources; record jurisdiction-dependent guidance separately.
3. **Manuscript drafting:** Develop substantive sections in sequence, keeping a cumulative word-count ledger.
4. **Application and visual development:** Add worked examples, exercises, diagrams, tables, and accessible figure explanations.
5. **Review:** Check subject coverage, factual accuracy, citations, ethics, developmental appropriateness, cultural assumptions, and consistency.
6. **PDF production:** Typeset the complete book with navigable headings, page numbers, contents, readable figures, and references.
7. **Final verification:** Extract and count PDF text, inspect layout, confirm the course identity, and check every acceptance criterion below.
8. **Delivery and register update:** Present the completed PDF and record its verified word count and completion status.

A collection of this size requires staged production. Partial work may be saved for continuity, but only a complete, verified book qualifies as a finished deliverable.

## Acceptance Checklist for Every Finished PDF

- Correct course code and exact course title.
- At least 150,000 words in the individual PDF, conservatively checked against substantive content.
- Coverage of every supplied area for that course, expanded into a coherent doctoral-level treatment.
- Simple-English lecture-style prose without loss of conceptual accuracy.
- Meaningful real-life applications and clearly identified fictional examples.
- Appropriate diagrams and illustrations, including subjects that benefit from visual explanation.
- Relevant integrative material included where mapped.
- Verified references; no invented sources, quotations, findings, or case evidence.
- Clear distinctions among evidence, interpretation, professional guidance, and local legal requirements.
- Respectful treatment of children, adolescents, caregivers, cultures, and disability.
- No confidential client information or unnecessary identifying details.
- Legible layout, usable contents and page references, selectable text, and complete figure captions.
- No audio files or expectation that the reader obtain a voice recording.
- A final word-count record tied to the actual delivered PDF.
