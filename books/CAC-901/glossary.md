# Working Glossary

These short definitions support the course lectures. They are starting points for study, not substitutes for the fuller explanations, research qualifications, or professional standards in the chapters.

**Accommodation.** Changing an existing idea or mental pattern when new information does not fit it. In assessment, the word can also mean changing how a task is accessed without changing the intended construct; context determines the meaning.

**Adaptation.** Adjustment to conditions or demands. In developmental work, adaptation should be judged in context and across more than one domain, not inferred only from compliance.

**Adolescence.** A developmental period involving biological, cognitive, social, and contextual changes. Its boundaries and expectations vary across cultures and institutions.

**Antecedent.** An event or condition that occurs before a behavior. It may help explain when the behavior is more likely, but temporal order alone does not establish its function.

**Assent.** A child’s affirmative agreement to participate when they cannot provide the full legally authorized consent. It should be informed, voluntary, and appropriate to the child’s understanding and applicable rules.

**Assimilation.** Interpreting new information through an existing idea or mental pattern. It is often discussed alongside accommodation in accounts of cognitive development.

**Attachment.** A developing pattern of emotional connection and organized behavior in a close caregiving relationship. It should not be reduced to a single moment of distress or a simple label for a child.

**Attrition.** Loss of participants during a study. It can reduce power and introduce bias if the people who leave differ systematically from those who remain.

**Baseline.** Information about functioning before an intervention or comparison period. A single baseline observation may not represent a stable pattern.

**Between-person difference.** Variation among individuals. It answers a different question from variation within one individual over time.

**Bias.** A systematic influence that can distort measurement, selection, interpretation, or decisions. Bias is not removed merely by collecting a large amount of data.

**Case formulation.** A reasoned account of a person’s concerns, strengths, context, and possible maintaining processes. It guides action and should be revised when new evidence appears.

**Causal inference.** A conclusion that one factor contributes to producing an outcome. It requires stronger evidence than association alone and depends on design and assumptions.

**Cohort.** A group that moves through time under partly shared historical conditions. Cohort membership should not be treated as a complete explanation of every member’s experience.

**Confidence interval.** A range produced by a statistical procedure that reflects uncertainty under its assumptions. It should not be interpreted as a guarantee about an individual child.

**Confidentiality.** The professional duty to protect information shared in a care or research relationship. Its limits should be explained and applicable law and ethical standards followed.

**Confound.** A factor associated with both an exposure and an outcome that can help explain an observed association. It is one reason correlation does not establish causation.

**Consequence.** An event following a behavior. Its effect on future behavior must be examined rather than assumed from the adult’s intention.

**Construct.** The concept a measure or theory is intended to represent, such as belonging or attention. A score is an indicator of a construct, not the construct itself.

**Co-regulation.** Support through a relationship that helps a person manage arousal and respond to demands. It is not the same as controlling or suppressing emotion.

**Correlation.** A pattern of association between variables. It does not by itself show that one variable causes the other.

**Critical period.** A limited interval during which particular experience may be especially necessary for a developmental process. Claims about critical periods should be domain-specific and evidence-based.

**Cross-sectional design.** A design comparing people at different ages or conditions at one time. It can be informative but does not directly observe individual change.

**Developmental cascade.** A process in which functioning in one domain influences later functioning in another domain or system. A proposed cascade requires evidence about timing and mechanism.

**Developmental plasticity.** The capacity for development to be modified. Plasticity is conditional and constrained, not unlimited change through effort.

**Developmental trajectory.** A pattern of stability and change over time. It describes a path and should not be treated as an inevitable destination.

**Dynamic assessment.** An approach that examines performance and response to guided support. Its uses and limits differ from standardized testing for a fixed decision.

**Ecological perspective.** An approach that locates development within connected settings, relationships, institutions, and social conditions.

**Effect size.** A quantitative description of the magnitude of a difference or association. Its practical meaning depends on the outcome, context, and uncertainty.

**Equifinality.** The possibility that different developmental pathways lead to a similar outcome.

**Executive functions.** Processes involved in organizing and regulating goal-directed activity, including planning, inhibition, shifting, and monitoring. Definitions vary across research traditions.

**Functional behavior assessment.** A process for developing hypotheses about the conditions and consequences that maintain a behavior. It should inform support rather than merely label the child.

**Generalization.** Use of a learned skill across settings, people, or conditions. It usually needs planning and should not be assumed from success in one session.

**Heritability.** A population-level estimate concerning variation associated with genetic differences in a particular population and environment. It does not describe a fixed proportion of an individual’s trait.

**Implementation fidelity.** The degree to which a program is delivered as intended. It should be considered alongside appropriate adaptation and actual access.

**Informed permission.** Authorization from a parent or legally authorized representative for a child’s research participation, subject to applicable law and ethics review.

**Longitudinal design.** A design that measures the same people repeatedly over time. It can describe change and temporal order but does not automatically establish causality.

**Measurement invariance.** The extent to which a measure represents the same construct across groups or occasions. It matters when scores are compared over development or context.

**Multifinality.** The possibility that a similar starting condition or exposure is followed by different outcomes.

**Multimethod assessment.** Use of more than one method or information source to answer an assessment question. Disagreement should be interpreted, not automatically averaged away.

**Negative reinforcement.** An increase in behavior associated with removal or avoidance of an aversive condition. It is not the same as punishment.

**Neurodiversity.** A perspective that recognizes variation in neurodevelopment while allowing attention to disability, support needs, identity, and distress.

**Norm-referenced score.** A score interpreted relative to a reference sample. Its usefulness depends on the relevance and quality of that sample and the intended decision.

**Operational definition.** A clear statement of how a variable or behavior is identified and measured. It supports consistent observation and communication.

**Plasticity constraint.** A biological, historical, or contextual limit on the change that is possible under particular conditions.

**Positive reinforcement.** An increase in behavior associated with the addition of a consequence. Whether something functions as reinforcement is shown by the pattern, not by its name.

**Protective factor.** A resource or condition associated with better adaptation, especially under adversity. Its effect depends on context and should not be treated as a guarantee.

**Random assignment.** Allocation of participants to conditions by a chance process. It can reduce systematic selection differences in an experiment.

**Random sampling.** Selection of participants from a population by a chance process. It concerns who enters a study and is distinct from random assignment.

**Reliability.** The consistency or precision of measurement. A reliable measure is not necessarily valid for every interpretation or decision.

**Resilience.** A dynamic process of positive adaptation in relation to adversity, supported by interacting personal, relational, and environmental resources.

**Risk factor.** A characteristic or condition associated with increased likelihood of an outcome. It is probabilistic, not a diagnosis or destiny.

**Scaffolding.** Temporary support that helps a person perform a task or use a skill, with adjustment as competence grows.

**Sensitive period.** A time when experience may have a stronger influence on a particular developmental process. It is usually less absolute than a critical period.

**Setting event.** A broader condition that changes the likelihood or meaning of an immediate behavior sequence, such as fatigue or prior conflict.

**Social validity.** The acceptability and meaningfulness of goals, procedures, and outcomes to the people affected. Definitions vary, so the intended use should be stated.

**Standardization.** Consistent procedures for administering and scoring a measure. It supports comparability but does not by itself establish validity.

**Transactional model.** An account of development in which the person and context influence one another over time.

**Trauma-informed practice.** Care that attends to safety, trust, choice, collaboration, and the possibility of trauma-related distress without assuming that every difficulty has the same cause.

**Validity.** Evidence supporting an interpretation or use of a measure or decision. It is not a permanent property that a test possesses in every context.

**Within-person change.** Variation within the same individual across time or situations. It should be distinguished from differences among individuals.

**Zone of proximal development.** A concept associated with Vygotsky concerning the difference between what a learner can do independently and what becomes possible with appropriate assistance.
