# CHAPTER 35
## Medications That Affect Sexual and Urinary Function: What to Know, What to Ask

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you should be able to: identify the major medication-classes whose sexual and urinary side-effects are clinically significant — antihypertensives, antidepressants, 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors, anticholinergics, opioids, and statins — with mechanism-specific understanding of each effect; distinguish medication-caused dysfunction from disease-caused dysfunction through timeline-and-challenge-dechallenge reasoning; apply the medication-review framework for comprehensive evaluation of sexual and urinary complaints; evaluate the management options (dose-adjustment, timing-modification, class-switch, and adjunctive-treatment) with evidence-based standards; navigate the prescriber-conversation's structure for reporting side-effects and requesting alternatives without treatment-interruption; and integrate medication-awareness into this book's comprehensive sexual-and-urinary-health architecture.
