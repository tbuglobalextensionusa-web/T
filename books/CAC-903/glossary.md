# Glossary

**Adaptive functioning:** Everyday skills needed for personal independence and social responsibility, including communication, self-care, and safety.

**Assent:** A child's affirmative agreement to participate in assessment, explained in developmentally appropriate language.

**Base rate:** The proportion of a population that has a condition or outcome. Base rates affect predictive values.

**Behavioral coding:** Systematic recording of behavior using defined categories, intervals, or events.

**Case formulation:** An organized hypothesis linking assessment findings to development, context, maintaining factors, and intervention.

**Clinical utility:** The degree to which assessment improves decisions, services, or client outcomes.

**Confidence interval:** A range around a score that reflects measurement uncertainty.

**Construct:** A concept or characteristic being measured, such as anxiety or working memory.

**Content validity:** Evidence that a measure adequately samples the domain it claims to assess.

**Criterion validity:** Evidence that scores relate to an external standard or outcome.

**Cut score:** A score threshold used to classify or make a decision.

**Differential assessment:** The process of distinguishing among possible explanations for a presenting concern.

**Dynamic assessment:** Assessment that examines learning potential through teaching, mediation, or supported performance.

**Evidence-based assessment:** Use of research and theory to select targets, methods, and interpretations.

**False negative:** A case that has the condition but is not identified by the assessment.

**False positive:** A case identified by the assessment that does not have the condition.

**Formative construct:** A construct formed by its indicators, which may not correlate strongly.

**Functional behavioral assessment:** Analysis of antecedents, behavior, and consequences to identify the function of behavior.

**Generalizability theory:** A framework for estimating how scores depend on persons, items, occasions, raters, and settings.

**Incremental validity:** Evidence that a method adds useful information beyond existing data.

**Informant:** A person who provides assessment information, such as a child, caregiver, or teacher.

**Internal consistency:** The degree to which items on a measure correlate as indicators of a reflective construct.

**Inter-rater reliability:** Agreement between different observers or raters.

**Measurement error:** Variation in observed scores that is not due to the construct being measured.

**Measurement invariance:** Evidence that a measure operates similarly across groups.

**Multi-informant assessment:** Use of reports from multiple people to capture behavior across contexts.

**Negative predictive value:** The probability that a negative screen is a true non-case.

**Norm-referenced interpretation:** Comparing a score to a reference group.

**Percentile:** A score indicating the percentage of the norm group scoring at or below a given point.

**Positive predictive value:** The probability that a positive screen is a true case.

**Predictive bias:** When a score predicts an outcome differently across groups.

**Progress monitoring:** Repeated brief assessment used to evaluate change and guide intervention.

**Projective hypothesis:** The idea that ambiguous stimuli may reveal inner experience; requires cautious interpretation.

**Reflective construct:** A construct that causes its indicators, which should correlate.

**Reliability:** Consistency or dependability of measurement.

**Reliable change:** Change that exceeds expected measurement error.

**Screening:** Brief assessment to identify possible concerns and need for further evaluation.

**Sensitivity:** The proportion of true cases correctly identified.

**Specificity:** The proportion of true non-cases correctly identified.

**Standard error of measurement:** An estimate of variability around an observed score due to measurement error.

**Standardized test:** A measure with uniform administration, scoring, and often norms.

**Test security:** Protection of test items, protocols, and data to preserve validity and privacy.

**Validity:** Evidence supporting the interpretation and use of scores for a specific purpose.
