# Applied Workbook: Assessment-to-Action Exercises

Use fictional or de-identified cases. Do not administer or interpret restricted tests without training, authorization, and supervision. These exercises practice reasoning, not unsupervised testing.

## Exercise 1: Referral question rewrite
Take a vague referral such as "behavior problem." Rewrite it as an assessable question. List three methods and three informants. State one alternative hypothesis.

## Exercise 2: Consent and assent script
Write a consent explanation for a caregiver and an assent explanation for an eight-year-old. Include purpose, privacy limits, and choice.

## Exercise 3: Cultural formulation
Write five questions for a family from a different background. Include meaning, language, supports, power, and barriers. Write one assumption to avoid.

## Exercise 4: Construct map
Choose a construct such as school refusal. List observable indicators. Decide whether the construct is reflective, formative, or mixed. Explain your reasoning.

## Exercise 5: Score explanation
Explain a percentile, standard score, and confidence interval to a caregiver in plain language. Include what the score does not mean.

## Exercise 6: Reliability audit
Choose a measure. Identify the reliability evidence most relevant to your decision. Write how much error is acceptable and why.

## Exercise 7: Validity argument
Write the interpretation you want to make from a test. List three validity supports and two threats. State whether the assessment will help the child.

## Exercise 8: Fairness review
Review a decision pathway in your setting. Identify who may be disadvantaged. Add two safeguards.

## Exercise 9: Interview plan
Write a semi-structured interview outline for school refusal. Include safety, strengths, context, and developmental adaptation.

## Exercise 10: ABC observation
Choose a behavior. Write an ABC hypothesis. Design an observation plan with definitions, setting, and coding method.

## Exercise 11: Cognitive demand analysis
Choose a classroom behavior. List two cognitive demands that might explain it. Design a brief assessment plan.

## Exercise 12: Intellectual and adaptive integration
Write why a full-scale score alone is insufficient. List three additional sources and how they would change interpretation.

## Exercise 13: Multi-informant discrepancy
Write a case where child and parent reports disagree. Generate three explanations. Write how you would explore them.

## Exercise 14: Expressive guardrails
Write three rules for using play or art responsibly. Include consent, interpretation limits, and documentation.

## Exercise 15: Structured judgment
Write a prediction. List data and a simple decision rule. State how you would check accuracy.

## Exercise 16: Screening pathway
Design a two-stage screening pathway. Identify sensitivity, specificity, base rate, and follow-up steps.

## Exercise 17: Monitoring plan
Choose a target, measure, frequency, feedback method, and decision rule. Include a plan for missing data.

## Exercise 18: Digital consent
Write a consent statement for a teen using a mood app. Include privacy, data use, and alternatives.

## Exercise 19: Feedback summary
Write a one-paragraph caregiver feedback summary. Include strength, concern, uncertainty, and next step.

## Exercise 20: Integrated case formulation
Write a full formulation for a fictional case. Include referral question, methods, findings, cultural context, ethics, recommendations, and monitoring.

## Portfolio guidance
Collect your best exercises with feedback and revisions. A doctoral portfolio should show growth in reasoning, humility, and ethical decision-making. Keep all client information de-identified.
